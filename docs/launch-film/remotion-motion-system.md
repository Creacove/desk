# Meet Your Manager · Remotion motion system

This film is not edited as a sequence of screenshots. It is one continuous 46-second choreography rendered at 60 fps.

## Core rule

There should be no intentional dead visual time.

At any given moment, at least one of the following is changing:

- camera position or scale;
- text reveal, mask or position;
- product surface position or scale;
- card/object depth, rotation or parallax;
- UI state;
- cursor position when a human decision matters;
- background light/grid position;
- shared object transition from one product state into the next.

Movement must still communicate meaning. Constant motion does not mean random motion.

## Motion grammar

### Camera

- push in: a decision or exact instruction becomes important;
- pull out: Desk has completed or assembled work;
- horizontal travel: reality changed and Desk has changed the route;
- center collapse: separate operating work resolves back into Desk;
- no decorative zooms that do not communicate a state change.

### Product objects

Objects should enter with physical weight using spring motion, then keep a subtle drift rather than becoming perfectly static.

Created work does not fade into a list. It physically emerges from the Manager surface, gains depth, then collapses into Files.

The old schedule does not disappear and a new schedule appear. The old route travels out while the new route enters through the same movement.

### Type

Major text uses masked reveals, translation and scale. It should not behave like subtitles.

Type can become part of the transition. For example, a product state may leave behind one word that carries into the next scene.

### Cursor

The cursor appears only when the human is exercising authority. The primary example is Approve & run.

Desk-driven work must continue without a cursor so the film visually distinguishes human authority from Manager autonomy.

## 46-second master timeline

The sequences overlap. The timestamps are edit anchors, not hard slide boundaries.

### 0:00 to ~0:04 · The question

Release-work fragments drift in while the camera slowly advances. “You make the music.” reveals through a vertical mask. “Who runs everything else?” enters before the first sentence has fully settled. The fragmented work begins collapsing before the next sequence starts.

### ~0:03 to ~0:10 · Give Desk the goal

The Manager surface rises into the same camera move. The artist request types in character by character. Send has a short physical response. Desk’s reply begins entering before the goal surface has finished its move.

### ~0:09 to ~0:16 · Understand and decide

Release-world objects orbit Odaeshi with persistent micro-motion. They gather toward the center while Today simultaneously pushes up from below. Context elements fall away as Today takes visual priority.

### ~0:14 to ~0:26 · Desk does the work

The Manager surface continues the camera push. EPK, press release, content plan and playlist pitch emerge at staggered intervals. Each object has independent spring, rotation and drift. As the camera pulls through the created work, the objects stack and physically resolve into Files.

### ~0:23 to ~0:31 · Exact human work

The Files collapse becomes the Mission Task surface. The camera continues inward. Hook, setup, shot, edit and CTA reveal independently. The Start action remains subtly alive rather than sitting still.

### ~0:30 to ~0:37 · Reality changes

The artist’s constraint arrives as a moving conversation object. The current calendar route travels left. Sunday’s route travels in from the right. This is the strongest horizontal camera move in the film because horizontal motion means the plan changed.

### ~0:35 to ~0:41 · Desk watches

The finished content becomes a post object while a watch system grows around it. Watch rings continuously breathe. Signal chips enter at different timings and continue subtle orbital/drift movement.

### ~0:39 to ~0:45 · Approve and run

The approval surface pushes toward camera. The cursor appears for the first time, enters from outside the composition and depresses Approve & run. The exact-effect card then changes state into the recorded provider outcome and next continuation.

### ~0:41 to 0:46 · Meet your manager

Product objects are still moving as they collapse toward the center. They resolve into the Desk mark. The final line arrives only after the operating system has visually become one object:

**Meet your manager.**

## Format direction

The same Remotion timeline drives all formats, but layout is recomposed per format.

- 9:16 is the primary composition and uses the tightest product crops.
- 4:5 keeps more horizontal product information for feed viewing.
- 16:9 uses wider spatial choreography and allows product and type to share the frame.

No format is created by cropping another format.

## Rendering

- 60 fps
- Vertical composition: 2160 × 3840
- Feed composition: 2160 × 2700
- Landscape composition: 3840 × 2160

The browser `/launch-film` route embeds the same Remotion composition through Remotion Player. It is not a separate autoplay implementation.

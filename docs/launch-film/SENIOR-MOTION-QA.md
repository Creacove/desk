# Meet Your Manager — Senior Motion QA Gate

A scene does not ship because it animates. It ships only when it survives this review.

## 1. Story
- One clear idea owns each moment.
- The viewer can explain what Desk just did without reading a caption.
- Every scene advances the same artist/release story.
- No feature appears solely because it exists in the product.
- No beta, traction or investor proof appears in this film.

## 2. Product truth
- Use the real Desk component when a production component already exists.
- Film-only adapters may freeze data or expose a state, but cannot invent a new product interaction.
- Manager, Today and Mission states must use production language and visual primitives.
- External media represents the artist/reality, never a fake Desk capability.

## 3. Focus
- One focal target per frame.
- On 9:16, the active product target should usually occupy 55–82% of safe frame width.
- Important product copy must remain readable at normal phone viewing size.
- If a label is too small, move the camera. Do not solve it by adding a duplicate headline.
- Background UI may remain visible only when it helps orientation. Otherwise soften it with scale, opacity, depth or crop.

## 4. Motion hierarchy
- Primary motion belongs to the object carrying the story.
- Secondary motion supports depth and continuity.
- Decorative motion never competes with product information.
- At least one meaningful property is changing through active beats: position, scale, crop, mask, content, depth, rotation, opacity or state.
- Avoid idle floating as a substitute for direction.

## 5. Camera
- Push in: the viewer needs to inspect or decide.
- Pull out: Desk completed work or context expands.
- Lateral move: plan/reality changes.
- Camera motion must land on a specific interface target.
- No random zooms. No generic Ken Burns movement.

## 6. Transitions
- A transition must inherit an object, shape, crop, line, image or motion vector from the previous scene whenever possible.
- Scene changes should feel like continuation, not page changes.
- Product objects may overlap transitions. Competing headlines may not.
- Avoid full-screen crossfades except for a deliberate emotional reset.
- Prefer morph, container resize, mask travel, object handoff, camera pass-through or match cut.

## 7. Real media
- Footage has a narrative role and a source/credit record.
- Media crops must protect faces, hands, microphones and phones from UI-safe-zone collisions.
- Footage should be graded into the light Desk system rather than dropped into arbitrary dark cards.
- Stock clips should appear as artist-world evidence, phone posts, EPK imagery or observable audience reality.

## 8. Light-mode art direction
- Default canvas: warm off-white / white.
- Product UI: production light theme.
- Accent: restrained Desk purple.
- Black is used for text, device edges or short contrast moments, not as the default environment.
- Avoid neon gradients, glowing AI orbs, starfields and generic futuristic textures.

## 9. Typography
- Product typography stays product typography.
- Film typography is sparse and should never restate what the product already makes obvious.
- Headlines enter and leave as designed objects, not subtitles.
- No more than one headline-level message owns a frame.

## 10. Interaction
- Cursor appears only when human authority/action matters.
- Do not animate tutorial-style cursor movement through normal navigation.
- Clicks have physical response: depression, state change and consequence.
- Autonomous Desk work should happen without a visible cursor.

## 11. Sound-ready motion
- Every major hit has an intended sound role: impact, tactile click, soft swipe, paper/document motion, notification, or silence.
- Do not cut visual timing before considering the sound attack/decay.
- Silence is allowed as emphasis.

## 12. Frame review
For each key beat inspect:
- first readable frame
- strongest hero frame
- transition midpoint
- last readable frame
- first frame of the next beat

Reject any frame with:
- two competing focal messages
- unreadable active UI
- accidental crop of the target
- meaningless empty space
- fake product representation where a real component exists
- obvious stock-footage feel with no product relationship
- visible motion that does not serve hierarchy

## 13. Mobile test
Before full render, export representative 540x960 and 1080x1920 frames/video.
Judge at phone size, not only on a desktop monitor.

## 14. Reference bar
The craft target is the product-motion discipline visible in strong Calendly, Linear, Raycast, Framer, Arc and Ordinary Folk launch work: continuity, object-led transitions, readable product, purposeful typography, strong art direction and no dead frames.
